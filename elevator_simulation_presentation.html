NEAREST CAR

Nearest Car (default)

Assigns each request to the elevator with the lowest estimated wait + travel time, following the actual SCAN path rather than straight-line distance.

Wins: 7 of 11 scenarios — strongest default choice across all traffic types

ROUND ROBIN

Round Robin (bonus)

Cycles through elevators in order regardless of floor position. Distributes requests evenly, preventing any single elevator from being overwhelmed.

Wins: over-provisioned fleets and express + regular hybrid setups

ZONE BASED

Zone Based (bonus)

Divides floors into equal contiguous zones — each elevator exclusively serves passengers whose origin falls in its zone.

Wins: bidirectional traffic when floors naturally separate by direction

When to use each algorithm

Recommendations based on simulation results across all 11 scenarios. Real-world traffic patterns map closely to the office scenarios below.

Nearest Car — default choice

Best when passenger pickups are spread across multiple floors and traffic volume is high enough that efficient routing matters. Its cost estimator accounts for direction, so it naturally avoids sending elevators on unnecessary detours.

Use when

Morning rush (all 15 done) Lunchtime (all 15 done) CEO visit (all 16 done) Town hall (fastest avg) Capacity pressure Mixed or unpredictable traffic

Round Robin — fairness first

Best when the building has more elevator capacity than it typically needs. RR prevents any single elevator from monopolising all requests, which matters in over-provisioned fleets or hybrid setups where an express elevator must stay available.

Use when

Over-provisioned fleet Express + regular hybrid Low or steady traffic Fairness across elevators matters Predictable, evenly distributed load

Zone Based — structured buildings

Best when the building is organised by department or function per floor range, so traffic naturally stays within zones. Also performs well when traffic is dispersed across floors heading in the same direction (e.g. lunchtime, where it achieves the lowest avg total time). Performs poorly whenever a large group originates from the same zone or floor — all requests pile onto one elevator regardless of capacity.

Use when

Floors grouped by department Bidirectional zoned traffic Predictable zone-level demand

Avoid when

Many passengers share a floor Morning rush / lunchtime surges Capacity is tight

Nearest Car wins 7 of 11 — but not always cleanly

NC is the safest default across all traffic types. The lunchtime scenario reveals a real tradeoff: Zone Based achieves a lower avg total time (35 ticks vs NC's 39.4) by naturally assigning each zone's downward traffic to its own elevator — but it fails to complete 2 of 15 journeys. NC's 15/15 completion record makes it the practical winner.

More elevators can make things worse with NC

With 8 elevators and only 3 passengers, Nearest Car assigns everything to E0 since it starts at floor 1 — always the cheapest option. E1 through E7 never move, and one passenger misses the 101-tick window. Round Robin prevents this by distributing requests regardless of position.

Zone Based's failure mode is always the same

In four of eleven scenarios, Zone Based leaves passengers unserved. The cause is identical every time: all passengers originate from the same floor or zone, so one elevator handles every request while the others sit idle. Morning rush, CEO visit, same floor surge, and capacity overflow all trigger this exact pattern.
